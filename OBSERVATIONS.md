# Observations from My Track and Field Visualizations

I used these charts to compare athletes, schools, and conference groups across the 400m, 100H, 400H, and long jump from 2016–2026. My event files contain 12,617 recorded rows from 20 selected schools. I included every result that could be read as a positive time or distance, rather than only each athlete’s best result.

## Comparing athletes

I noticed that athletes have different numbers of recorded results and years in the data. For example, Alysia Johnson’s 400m records cover 2021–2025, while some athletes appear in only one season. Having more dots means I collected more results for that athlete; it does not automatically mean they performed better. To look for improvement, I need to follow the same athlete over time. Lower times show faster races, while higher distances show longer jumps. I cannot assume that an athlete’s first and last recorded years represent their whole college career.

## Comparing schools and conferences

All four event files include 20 schools, but the conference groups do not have equal numbers of records. The SEC group has the most 400m rows, with 903. The Big 12 group has the most rows in 100H, 400H, and long jump. I interpret these as differences in the results I collected, rather than evidence that one group is better. Schools with more recorded results contribute more dots. Also, the conference labels describe my project groups, rather than each school’s conference in every historical year.

## Comparing events and years

The 100H file has the most recorded rows, with 3,487, while the 400H file has the fewest, with 2,711. The busiest recorded year also differs by event: 100H has its highest count in 2026, 400H in 2023, 400m in 2021, and long jump in 2016. These differences could reflect collection coverage or participation, so they do not establish a change in performance.

The middle recorded results are 54.27 seconds for 400m, 13.77 seconds for 100H, 60.09 seconds for 400H, and 5.83 meters for long jump. These are medians across numeric records, not athlete averages. Athletes with more results have more influence on these summaries.

## What I need to check next

The files contain only two rows for 2020: one 400m result and one long-jump result. I should check those sources before explaining that year. I also noticed unusually large times, including 109.13 seconds in 400m and 46.10 seconds in 100H. I kept them, but need to review their source links before interpreting them. Most age and birth-date fields are missing, so I cannot make reliable age comparisons yet. My next step is to review unusual results, missing records, wind readings, and repeated entries before drawing stronger conclusions.
